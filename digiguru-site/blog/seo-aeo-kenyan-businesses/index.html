Search visibility

SEO and AEO in Kenya: How Businesses Get Found in Search

Understand the practical overlap between search engine optimisation and answer engine optimisation, and what Kenyan businesses should fix before chasing new tactics.

RMRobin MigwiFounder, DigiGuruPublished 21 October 20269 minute read

If someone searches for the service you provide, can they find a useful page on your website, understand why your business fits their needs and take the next step without guessing? That is the practical test behind SEO. AEO, usually short for answer engine optimisation, asks a related question: can your content clearly answer the questions people bring to search and other answer experiences?

For a Kenyan business, the best starting point is not a trick for appearing in an AI answer. It is making the business, its services, its location and its expertise easy for people and search systems to understand.

SEO and AEO: what is the difference?

Search engine optimisation (SEO) improves how a website can be discovered and understood through search. This includes relevant pages, clear titles and headings, technical accessibility, internal links, useful content and a good experience on mobile.

Answer engine optimisation (AEO) is a term commonly used for making content easier to use when people ask direct questions and expect an answer. In practice, the work often overlaps with good SEO: answer real questions clearly, use descriptive page structure and support claims with reliable information.

Google’s current guidance says the foundational SEO practices still apply to AI search experiences, and that there are no extra technical requirements or special schema types required just to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. Eligibility is not a promise that a page will be shown. Read Google’s guide to generative AI features in Search for its latest direction.

Start with the search behind the question

A search term only tells part of the story. Someone searching “dentist Lavington” may want a nearby clinic and opening hours. Someone asking “how long does a dental crown last” is trying to understand a treatment. A business page and an educational article serve different purposes, so the page should match the intent.

Write down the real questions customers ask before choosing you. Group them by intent:

  • Find a provider: service, location, opening hours, coverage area and how to contact the business.
  • Compare options: what is included, differences between options, price factors, timelines and who each option suits.
  • Understand a problem: explanations, trade offs, common mistakes and the steps a customer can take.
  • Take action: how to book, request a quote, place an order or speak to the team.

This gives each page a job. It also stops a business from trying to make one generic homepage rank for every service, question and location.

Build pages that are genuinely helpful

Give each important service a proper page

Explain what the service includes, who it is for, how the process works, what affects the cost and what the customer should do next. Use the terms customers naturally use, including relevant local language and place names where they genuinely apply. Avoid repeating “Nairobi” or “Kenya” in every sentence merely to force a keyword.

Answer questions directly, then add the context

Start a section with a clear answer. Follow it with the conditions, examples or details needed to make that answer accurate. If the answer varies by product, location or customer situation, say so. Short, direct explanations help people scan the page; meaningful detail gives them a reason to trust it.

Make trust visible

Include real business details, a clear author or business identity, accurate service information and evidence you can stand behind. Use real examples and explain what was done. Never create fictional reviews, awards, customer results or professional qualifications to make a page appear authoritative.

Make the page easy to crawl and navigate

Use one clear page title, a useful main heading, descriptive subheadings and links to related pages. Important content should be available as readable text, not only embedded in an image or hidden inside an interaction. Ensure that pages load properly on phones and that internal links let people find connected services and supporting articles.

A useful page testIf a potential customer lands directly on this page from search, can they explain what you do, who the service suits, why they should trust the information and what to do next? If not, improve the page before publishing more content.

Local SEO matters when location matters

For clinics, restaurants, retailers, trades and other businesses that serve particular areas, accurate local information can be central to the customer decision. Keep your Google Business Profile details current, including your business name, address or service area, hours, contact routes and appropriate categories. Make sure your website gives consistent information and explains which locations you actually serve.

Build local pages only when they represent meaningful differences for the customer. A real page for a branch can help a visitor understand that location’s services, team, directions and booking process. Copying one paragraph into dozens of near identical town pages gives customers little value.

What should you do for AI answers?

Focus on being a useful source, not on gaming the format of the answer. Make factual claims precise, explain important terms, show how to complete the task and cite trustworthy sources when you rely on outside facts. Provide original detail from your work or experience where you can.

Do not add structured data that does not match visible content, manufacture mentions, or assume that adding an FAQ block or a new machine readable file will guarantee AI visibility. Google’s guidance specifically emphasises helpful, unique content and established technical practices over special “AEO hacks”. Its advice on helpful, reliable, people first content is a good editorial standard.

Measure what happens after discovery

Search traffic is a useful signal, but it is not the business outcome by itself. Use Search Console to understand which queries and pages receive impressions and clicks, where visibility changes and which content earns attention. Then inspect the journey on your own site: do visitors browse a relevant service, submit an enquiry, start a conversation or book?

Review the data after pages have had time to be discovered. Search changes may take weeks or months to become clear, and no SEO consultant can honestly guarantee a particular ranking. Improve the page based on what people need and what the evidence shows.

A practical first month

  1. Week one: list your priority services, service areas and the questions customers ask before purchasing.
  2. Week two: improve the homepage and most commercially important service pages with accurate detail, useful headings and clear next steps.
  3. Week three: publish one in depth article that answers a real customer question and links naturally to the appropriate service page.
  4. Week four: check Search Console, test key mobile journeys and improve any page where intent and next action are unclear.

SEO and AEO work best when they connect discovery with the customer experience after the click. Being found creates the chance to help. A useful page and a clear path give that chance somewhere to go.

Common questions

Is AEO different from SEO?

AEO usually refers to improving how content answers direct questions in search and answer experiences. Much of the practical work overlaps with SEO, including useful information, clear structure, accessible pages and strong technical foundations.

Can a small Kenyan business appear in AI search results?

A site can be eligible if it meets the relevant Search requirements, but appearance is not guaranteed. Focus on accurate, useful, distinctive content and make sure the pages you want discovered are accessible and internally linked.

How long does SEO take to work?

There is no fixed timeline. Search engines need to crawl and evaluate changes, and results vary by query, competition, site condition and the quality of the work. Track progress over time rather than expecting instant results.
